# Job-sim games (CaseOh style): what works
Date: 2026-10-20

Sourcing note: web results were thin and sometimes conflicting. Things marked **(general knowledge)** or **(guess)** are not from a fetched source. Check live Steam pages before quoting numbers.

## The genre in one paragraph
You play ONE person running a small business in first person. You do every job by hand (stock shelves, scan items, clean), earn cash, and unlock more stuff. Streamers love it because it is easy to understand in 10 seconds, has constant small tasks, and customers do silly things.

## Shared loop pattern
1. **Tiny start.** Empty or broken place, little cash, few items. (general knowledge)
2. **Manual tasks.** You do everything by hand, so you feel busy.
3. **Cash in, spend on a visible upgrade.** Each purchase changes what the place looks like or what you can do.
4. **Staff later.** Hiring helpers removes chores and shifts you to bigger decisions.
5. **Expansion by unlock gates.** Level or license gates control the pace. Supermarket Simulator uses store level and licenses for product types **(general knowledge)**.

## Satisfying moments (why people watch)
- Number go up: cash popping, level-up banners, a till "ka-ching".
- Tidy fantasy: perfectly lined-up shelves, clean tile (PowerWash).
- Surprise reveal: opening a pack, a rare item.
- Speed and flow: fast scanning of a long line.
- Before/after: dirty to clean, run-down to shiny.

## NPC behaviors that create funny or stressful moments
- Queues grow and customers get angry when you are slow. Supermarket Simulator customers complain when prices are too high ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/0/677329154824225463/?ctp=2)).
- Shoplifters, customers who block aisles, ones who ask for odd things. **(general knowledge)**
- Physics goofs: items flying, NPCs stuck. Streamers clip these. Keep some jank charming, but not blocking.

## Money, prices, upgrades
- Player sets prices; too high = customers leave or complain, too low = slow growth. Players love "sweet spot" tuning.
- Upgrades come in 3 types: capacity (more customers), speed (checkout faster), automation (staff). **(general knowledge)**
- Price-per-purchase should rise slowly so early buys feel affordable.

## Common complaints (from reviews/analysis)
- Price vs content: some find ~$20 high ([review analysis](https://vaporlens.app/app/2961880/grocery_store_simulator) discusses a sibling title; treat as indicative).
- Paid DLC packs seen as small and pricey, while bugs remain.
- Bugs, frame drops, stuck workers/NPCs.
- Economy that stalls (money piles up with nothing to buy, or no cash to progress). Reported in early TCG reviews.
- Repetition late game. **(general knowledge)**

## Scope and price
- Typical price $10-25; many launch in Early Access and sell millions for cheap-ish dev budgets. Several were made by tiny teams. Only about 21% of games leaving Early Access earned more in their first 30 days at 1.0 than in Early Access, per [one 2026 analysis](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-early-access-graduation) (single source).
- Minimum shape: one building, 1-3 core jobs, 20-50 unlockable items, 10+ hours.

## Ideas for our game
- **Step 2:** Give the first surfer a visible patience meter or emoji above the head (happy/neutral/annoyed). Easy to read on stream. Add a short "line forms at the desk" moment.
- **Step 2:** Make the first ride a clear payoff: camera shake, crowd cheer, a "+$" pop over the surfer.
- **Step 3:** Let the player set ticket price with a sweet spot. Too high = fewer surfers arrive and some complain with a speech bubble.
- **Step 3:** Money HUD pops numbers and plays a register sound at the desk.
- **Step 3:** Buy upgrades that change how the pool looks (new sign, new bench), not just stats.
- **Step 4+:** Run-down to shiny arc: dirty tiles, broken lights, repaired one by one (PowerWash + Gas Station fantasy).
- **Step 4+:** Hire a lifeguard/ticket clerk as the "staff unlock".
- **Step 4+:** Wipeout ragdolls as clip-worthy goofs; keep them harmless and fast to reset.
- **Later:** Keep content in the base game rather than many small paid packs (a top review complaint); fix economy stalls before adding more content.
