# How real surf parks are built and laid out
Date: 2026-10-20

Sourcing: numbers marked (claim) come from the company; (guess) is mine; (general) is common industry knowledge I did not verify. Sources disagree a lot, so treat sizes as rough. Technology names are explained in [wave-pools.md](wave-pools.md); wave shaping basics in [wave-physics.md](wave-physics.md).

Depth (general, guess): park pools are mostly 1-4 m deep near the generator, sloping up to a beach with a gentle 1:10 to 1:20 slope. I found no verified depth profile; most numbers are secret.

## Bathymetry (shape of the bottom)
Bathymetry = the shape and slope of the pool floor; it decides how a wave breaks ([CDC definition](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/toolkit/surf-venue-guidance.html)).
- **Reef = built ramp:** a raised concrete block area. When the wave hits it, it gets taller and breaks. Early pools used one baffle (a low wall) as a fake reef.
- **Steep, sudden reef** gives a plunging, hollow wave (barrel, advanced). **Long gentle slope** gives a spilling wave (easy, beginners). Wavegarden's newer reef layouts offer barreling and air-section waves, plus a closeout end for foam or small tube finishes ([Wavegarden Lab](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/wavegarden-creates-wave-pool-lab-and-high-performance-surfing-center)).
- **Diagonal reef:** one side shallower makes the break travel sideways, so you can ride along the wave (peeling).
- **Zones** (general): outer/reef = advanced and steeper; middle = intermediate; inside/beach = beginner whitewater and foam. Different zones can run different wave sizes at the same time.
- **Channels:** deeper lanes along the edge let surfers paddle back to the start without crossing the break (general). Often a walkway or "return lane" at the side.
- Fan layouts need two sides to match, so the wave pier is symmetrical; a long single-sided pool allows only one direction.

## The rest of the park
Architects say the wave technology drives the surrounding plan, and suggest a ~75 ft (~23 m) zone next to the beach should feel connected to the action ([Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/how-does-technology-footprint-affect-layout-surf-park-design-insights-from-two-architects/)).
- **Typical zones (general):**
  1. Arrival: parking, entrance, check-in desk.
  2. Hub building: changing rooms, lockers, showers, rental and shop, cafe/bar.
  3. Beach/lawn and deck right next to the pool, with shade and seating.
  4. Surf school area at the shallow inside edge.
  5. Back-of-house (pumps, filtration, storage), kept out of sight.
- **Flow:** park, check in, rent a board/wetsuit, change, walk to the lagoon edge, wait in the lineup/queue, surf, shower, eat, shop. Put cafe and viewing deck by the water so waiting friends and parents stay (and spend).
- **Lifeguards:** towers/platforms at the water edge, often on the pier or beach ends, watching the whole lineup (general).
- **Lighting:** floodlights on poles around the lagoon allow night sessions; many parks run late (general; not verified).
- **Landscaping:** The Wave Bristol sits in gardens, woodland and meadow with clubhouse, cafe, bar and shop ([Soglos summary](https://www.soglos.com/business-profile/outdoors/the-wave/16175)). Tropical parks use palms and sand.

## Building and running
- **Build cost:** USD ~10-33 million for the engine plus pool; 50-75 million for large, amenity-heavy parks (see [wave-pools.md](wave-pools.md)). URBNSURF Melbourne: AUD 18.5 million or 28.3 million depending on the source (they don't agree).
- **Time to build:** a year or two of concrete, plumbing and testing, plus approvals (guess; Bristol had planning in 2015 and opened in 2019).
- **Water:** filtered and disinfected like a big pool; guidance covers filtration and injury/drowning risks ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/toolkit/surf-venue-guidance.html)). Surfers and algae make cleaning a daily job (general).
- **Energy:** biggest running cost (general; I found no reliable figures). Bigger waves and more waves/hour use more power.
- **Maintenance downtime:** wave modules, foils and pumps need regular servicing, usually off-hours or closed days (general).
- **Hiring the whole lagoon:** URBNSURF lists from AUD 1,250/hour, a real revenue idea ([URBNSURF](https://urbnsurf.com/melbourne/?p=4747)).

## Ideas for our game
- **Different pools:** unlockable pool layouts, each a new map:
  - **Starter Rectangle** (what we have): one wave, one direction at a time.
  - **Cove Fan:** center pier, waves left and right at the same time; two lineups. Plays like "double the capacity, double the staff".
  - **Long Lagoon (Ranch style):** very long, one perfect barrel; few surfers, very high ticket price, pro events. Plays like a luxury low-volume park.
  - **Round Lake (Surf Lakes style):** central plunger, 4 reefs around the edge each with its own lineup; wave menu per reef. Plays like multitasking.
  - **Sheet-wave corner (FlowRider style):** tiny, cheap add-on for kids and tourists, no peeling. Later.
- **Better world:** build pool shape with a few colored zones: reef blocks, beach slope, return channel. Sand beach, lawn, palms, deck with benches, lifeguard tower, light poles.
- **Different pools:** bathymetry upgrades: "Steeper reef" (hollower, pro waves), "Gentler slope" (easier beginner waves), "Diagonal reef" (longer peel), "Return channel" (more waves per surfer per hour), "Closeout section" (foam finish).
- **Better world:** park layout items to place: parking, hub building (changing rooms, rentals, cafe), viewing deck, surf school corner, back-of-house with pumps. Flow idea: surfers walk the real path, so bad layout = long walk = low happiness.
- **Later:** night sessions with floodlights (extra hours, cost power); lagoon hire for groups; cleaning/filtration chore; pump maintenance downtime.
- **Later:** park score rewards a nice look near the water (deck, plants) using the ~23 m "action zone" idea.
