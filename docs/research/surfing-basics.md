# Surfing basics for game design
Date: 2026-10-20

Sourcing note: most of this is common surf knowledge, not from a fetched source. Treat details as general knowledge and verify names/slang with a surfer if you can.

## Skill levels (a good progression ladder)
| Level | What they can do | Typical wave | Typical board |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Paddle, pop up, ride straight in whitewater | Small, foamy, slow | Foamie (soft top), big and floaty |
| Improver | Catch unbroken waves, turn a bit | Small green waves | Foamie or longboard |
| Intermediate | Trim along the wave, turn, cutbacks | Chest-high, peeling | Funboard / longboard / mid-length |
| Advanced | Fast turns, aerials, tubes | Overhead, fast, hollow | Shortboard |

## Boards
- **Foamie (soft top):** safe for beginners and lessons, very stable.
- **Longboard:** 8-10 ft, easy to catch waves, smooth style; older/chilled surfers.
- **Shortboard:** about 5-6.5 ft, fast and tricky; experts and kids who shred.
- Fun for game: board color and size tell the player skill at a glance.

## Gear
- **Wetsuit:** keeps warm; pools often rent them.
- **Leash:** cord that ties the board to the ankle; stops it from flying off.
- **Wax or traction pad:** grip on deck.
- Rash guard, sunscreen, fins for rental boards.

## Etiquette (great NPC drama)
- **Priority:** the surfer closest to the breaking part of the wave gets it.
- **Dropping in:** taking off on a wave someone else has priority on. The classic rude move.
- **Snaking:** paddling around someone to steal their spot.
- Don't ditch your board in someone's path; wait your turn in the lineup.
- In pools, staff manage rotation, so etiquette becomes "who is next", which fits our queue.

## What makes a ride good
Catching the wave on time, steady speed, going along the open face instead of just straight, a few turns, a long ride, and finishing with style (kickout). A long peeling wave beats a quick closeout.

## Common wipeouts (comedy list)
- Late takeoff: nose digs in ("pearling"), flips over the front.
- Catching a rail: edge of the board bites the water.
- Standing up too soon: falls backwards.
- Hit by the wave lip, "washing machine" tumble.
- Board hits you after the fall (the leash snaps it back).
- Lose the shorts or wetsuit zip: funny but keep it PG.

## Lesson structure (typical, general knowledge)
1. Safety talk and how to carry the board.
2. Practice popping up on the sand or deck.
3. Paddle technique.
4. Practice on whitewater with instructor pushing you.
5. Free surf and feedback.
About 60-90 minutes, groups of 4-8 per coach.

## Culture and flavor
- Vibe: relaxed, sunny, "stoked" (very happy), gnarly (intense, big or scary), "shred" (surf hard), "send it", "wipe out", "barrel/tube", "swell", "stoked", "lineup", "set" (group of waves), "kook" (beginner, often rude if used on others), "bro/dude".
- Music: surf rock (guitars with reverb), reggae, chill lo-fi, upbeat indie. Use a few looping tracks; check licensing.
- NPC types (guess): Kook Kevin (tries hard), Chill Dude (longboard, slow), Competitive Kid (shortboard, impatient), Cautious Parent, Influencer filming everything.

## Ideas for our game
- **Step 2:** One surfer model, foamie in a bright color; ride straight in whitewater first. Simple states: walk, paddle, stand, ride, wipeout.
- **Step 2:** Give each surfer a random skill level now (even if unused); later it picks which waves they buy.
- **Step 3:** Skill-based happiness: beginner on pro wave wipes out and is unhappy; right level = happy tip.
- **Step 4+:** Board rental counter with foamie / longboard / shortboard and a wetsuit option.
- **Step 4+:** Etiquette events: two surfers on the same wave = "drop-in" pause with a speech bubble; the player (as lifeguard) can fix it.
- **Step 4+:** Wipeout varieties (pearl, rail catch, washing machine) as short animation variants.
- **Step 4+:** Lessons: NPC coach and a group of beginner kooks.
- **Later:** Surfer personalities with slang bubbles ("stoked!", "gnarly!"), surf-rock radio, and a "stoke meter" per surfer.
