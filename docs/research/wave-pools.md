# Real wave pools and how they make money
Date: 2026-10-20

Sourcing note: search results were limited and many numbers are company claims. Marked **(guess)** where I fill gaps.

## Main technologies
| Tech | How it makes waves | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wavegarden Cove | Foils/blades pushed along a pool shape; waves break both ways across a lagoon | Used at The Wave Bristol, URBNSURF Sydney, Surfland Brasil and others ([Wavepool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/first-waves-roll-out-at-the-wave-bristol/), [Blooloop](https://blooloop.com/water-parks/news/wavegarden-surfland-brasil/)). Zones in the lagoon give different difficulty. |
| Surf Lakes | A big plunger in the middle of a round-ish lake sends rings out, waves break on reefs around the edge | Claims up to ~2,400 waves/hour; an engineering paper estimates ~2,000 surfable; ~200 surfers x ~10 waves/hour mentioned ([SurfToday](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/surf-lakes-is-already-making-waves-in-queensland), [EAP](https://www.eap-magazin.de/Article/Australia%3A-New-Multi-Break-Wave-Pool-Promises-Surfing-Fun-with-up-to-2-400-Waves-per-Hour.html)). Developer claims, not measured data. |
| American Wave Machines PerfectSwell | Pumps air in water to build waves; configurable shapes | Used at BSR Surf Resort, Waco, Texas ([SurfToday](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/bsr-surf-resort-promises-all-types-of-waves-in-texas)). Waves said to be shorter. |
| Kelly Slater Surf Ranch | A hydrofoil on a train runs along a long channel | Goal: ocean-like swell. Mostly a single perfect wave to the left and right with little else around it ([Wavepool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/insights-epicsurf-on-building-an-economically-sustainable-surf-park/)). |
| Others (guess/general knowledge) | Alaia Bay (wave foil in a flat pool), Endless Surf, etc. | Details not verified here. |

## Waves and session format
- Waves per surfer: roughly 10-12 per one-hour session at URBNSURF Sydney; a Virginia Beach developer said a keen surfer could get ~30/hour ([The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/surf/pharrells-proposed-virginia-beach-surf-park-overcomes-first-major-hurdle/)).
- A Portugal project claims 1,000 waves/hour; looks like marketing ([Idealista](https://www.idealista.pt/en/news/lifestyle-in-portugal/2026/07/17/76622-portugal-s-first-wave-pool-now-open-for-bookings)).
- Sessions are typically about 1 hour, booked in advance, split by skill level (**general knowledge**). Menus: beginner (soft, whitewater-ish or small green), intermediate, advanced/barrel. Lefts and rights are scheduled or shared across zones.
- Surfers wait in a lineup; one wave is shared by a few riders spread in zones (inside = easy, middle, outer = hard) **(general knowledge)**.

## Extra revenue
Coaching, equipment hire, photos/video, surf shop, food and drink, events, and in some parks accommodation ([Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/is-it-worth-it-the-risks-and-rewards-of-wave-pool-investment/)). Add-ons can rival session revenue. Kelly Slater's ranch keeps infrastructure minimal; Alaia Bay adds a chalet, dining and retail ([Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/insights-epicsurf-on-building-an-economically-sustainable-surf-park/)). Hotels: Surfers Cove Portugal has accommodation attached.

## Business numbers
- Build cost: roughly USD 10-33 million for the engine plus concrete pool, USD 50-75 million for large amenity-heavy parks, per sources in [Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/is-it-worth-it-the-risks-and-rewards-of-wave-pool-investment/) and [The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/features/wave-pool-expert-financial-state-industry/).
- Payback claims run from ~2 years (if fully booked) to 7-8 years.
- One licensor quoted operating cost around USD 600k/year; I did not find reliable energy figures.

## Staff roles
Lifeguards/safety (required), coaches/instructors, wave operator/technician, front desk/booking, rental and shop staff, cafe staff, cleaners. **(general knowledge)**

## Real problems
- Crowding and queues: design space for waiting guests ([ADG](https://www.aquaticgroup.com/news/88/Developing-the-Business-of-Surf/)).
- Revenue per person must hit a target; wave alone rarely carries the park.
- Wave consistency, equipment maintenance, energy cost, weather (cold, wind, rain, storms close sessions) **(general knowledge/guess, not verified)**.

## Ideas for our game
- **Step 2:** One wave per "session slot": surfer pays, queues at the pool edge, wave plays, they ride and leave. Wave counts per ticket start at 1 for now.
- **Step 3:** Ticket = time slot with N waves (e.g. 3 waves for $15). Price scales with wave difficulty.
- **Step 3:** First upgrades: second wave (so two surfers per session), better wave machine (faster recharge).
- **Step 4+:** Wave menu panel at the machine: Beginner / Intermediate / Pro, left/right, with price multipliers.
- **Step 4+:** Pool zones (inside = beginner, outside = advanced) with different ticket prices.
- **Step 4+:** Side revenue in order: board rental, snack bar, surf shop, lessons (coach NPC), photos.
- **Step 4+:** Energy bill each day; bigger waves cost more power.
- **Later:** Weather days, equipment breakdowns the player repairs, events (competition day), on-site hotel.
