# Surf maneuvers: what is possible on which wave
Date: 2026-10-21

Why this file: Ido said "on small waves you can't snap and do tricks, only in bigger ones. Learn how real surfing works more."
Short answer: **Ido is right.** In real life, small waves are where most tricks happen. Wave SIZE is not what unlocks a trick. **SPEED + a STEEP, BREAKING section** unlock tricks.

How sure am I? Sources are linked. Where I wrote from general surf knowledge (no source) I mark it "(my knowledge)". Wave-pool zone names/numbers are partly from company plans and vary by site.
Related files: [surfing-motion.md](surfing-motion.md) (body poses), [boards-and-tricks.md](boards-and-tricks.md), [surf-feel.md](surf-feel.md), [wave-pool-look.md](wave-pool-look.md).

## 1. The big idea: three ingredients, not "wave height"
| Ingredient | Plain meaning | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Board moving fast along the wave | Steep face + pumping (see below) |
| Open face | Unbroken green wall in front of you to carve on | Peeling wave (breaks gradually left or right) |
| A section | A part of the wave that is steep, curling, or about to close | Lip (top edge that falls), a hollow bit, foam ahead |

- A 2 ft wave that peels with a steep lip lets a good surfer snap, floater, tail-slide and air. A 6 ft wave that closes out (breaks all at once) allows almost nothing but a straight ride.
- What big waves add: more power and more speed, so moves get BIGGER (longer spray, higher airs) and barrels (tubes) become possible. They do not add new basic moves.
- What small waves take away: speed and power. Fix = pumping, light short boards (fish, small thruster) and smaller, tighter turns. Pumping is taught as the first speed skill because it lets you make your own speed instead of relying on the wave ([Hawaiian South Shore, pumping](https://www.hawaiiansouthshore.com/blogs/hawaiian-south-shore-surfing-blog/surf-maneuvers-series-part-2-pumping-down-the-line); sources agree on "speed, speed, speed").
- Floaters are actually taught on small, crumbly waves first: easier to learn there ([SurferToday, floater](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/how-to-pull-a-floater); [Barefoot Surf Travel](https://tutorials.barefootsurftravel.com/articles/how-to-do-a-floater/)).

## 2. Maneuvers by kind of wave
Legend: Y = normal, (y) = possible but small/simple or needs a good wave, - = not really.
Sizes are on the wave face (the front slope). Table is my knowledge unless stated.

| Maneuver | Whitewater (foam) | Small green 1-3 ft | Medium 3-5 ft | Big / hollow 6 ft+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pop-up, straight ride, kick-out | Y | Y | Y | Y (scary) |
| Angle / trim along the face | - (foam has no face; you just go straight, maybe a small angle) | Y | Y | Y |
| Pumping | - | Y (key skill) | Y | rarely needed |
| Bottom turn | - | Y (small, gentle) | Y | Y (big, powerful) |
| Top turn | - | Y | Y | Y |
| Snap / re-entry (hard turn on the lip, spray) | - | Y if there is a steep section; short, quick | Y | Y (big spray) |
| Cutback (turn back toward the power) | - | Y (roundhouse, slow and wide) | Y | Y |
| Floater (ride over the breaking lip) | - | Y (easiest place to learn) | Y | (y) risky, can be thrown |
| Foam climb | - | Y | Y | (y) |
| Tail slide / fin release (back of board slides out) | - | Y (small waves are the usual place) | Y | rare |
| Air / air reverse | - | Y for good surfers on a crumbly lip | Y | Y (higher) but top surfers prefer smaller, steep waves for airs |
| Full rotation, alley-oop, grabs (indy, stalefish) | - | (y) very good surfers | Y | Y |
| Layback (lean way back in a turn) | - | (y) | Y | Y |
| Tube ride (barrel) | - | (y) tiny "cover-ups" only | (y) | Y: this is the big wave's special thing |
| Noseride, hang five, hang ten (longboard) | - | Y: best on small clean waves | (y) | - |

Takeaway for the game: **only three things really need a big wave: a real barrel, the biggest airs, and the biggest power turns.** Snaps, cutbacks, floaters, tail slides and small airs belong on small waves too.

## 3. Wave pools
Pools make the size/difficulty choice for you by zones or "wave settings". Sources are a bit messy, so treat numbers as rough.
- **Wavegarden Cove**: lagoon split into beginner (near the shore), intermediate (middle) and advanced/expert (out the back). Planned Melbourne zones: beginner = knee-high rolling whitewater, intermediate = waist-high green waves breaking both ways, expert = biggest, longest, hardest ([Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/?p=3107), [Raised Water Research: Wavegarden Barcelona](https://raisedwaterresearch.com/spot/wave-pool/europe/spain/wavegarden-barcelona/)). Face heights reported: about 1.7 ft up to about 6.5 ft; "advanced" up to 5-6 ft ([Raised Water Research: The Wave](https://raisedwaterresearch.com/?p=275)). Setting codes like "T1/T2/T3/B1" appear for expert/advanced sessions at The Wave, Bristol (same source); I did not find an official sheet, so the "M3/M4/M5" and "Bay/Reef" names Ido mentioned are **unverified**. Wavegarden's lists claim 15-20+ wave profiles ([Wikipedia: Wavegarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavegarden), [Wave Pool Mag](https://wavepoolmag.com/?p=3107)).
- **Kelly Slater Surf Ranch**: one wave, about 2-6 ft, hollow and long (100+ m), left and right, built for performance and barrels. It is an "advanced/pro" product, not a beginner one ([Wave Pool Mag: Surf Ranch](https://wavepoolmag.com/?p=56)).
- **URBNSURF (Wavegarden Cove tech)**: beginner / intermediate / advanced lanes, as above.
- **American Wave Machines (PerfectSwell / Barrel Machine / Flowrider-style)**: not researched in depth. From my knowledge: PerfectSwell makes a small to medium peeling wave good for learning, shortboard turns and airs; the Barrel Machine is about a fixed standing tube.
- Pool takeaway: even the "intermediate" wave (waist-high green) is expected to let people carve and turn. Pool operators sell that as the main fun product. Expert settings are "steeper and faster", so moves that need a steep lip happen there.

## 4. The core maneuvers (body + board + when)
Icons: SPEED = needs speed, LIP = needs a steep lip/curl, SECTION = needs a section breaking or closing ahead.

| Move | What the body/board does | When in the wave | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pop-up | From lying, hands push the board, both feet jump under you in one move, land in a crouch, eyes forward | At take-off, as the wave lifts the board | none |
| Angling / trim | Stand low, weight a bit on the front foot, board slightly angled across the face, arms out | Right after take-off and on any open stretch | face |
| Pumping | Press the rails down the face then up it, front foot pushes, knees bend and straighten, like a skateboard "S" | Flat or slow parts, small waves | face |
| Bottom turn | Drop down the face, bend knees, lean, carve a curve at the bottom, then climb back up | First move after take-off; sets up everything else | SPEED |
| Top turn | Climb to the top third and turn the board back down | After the bottom turn, mid-ride | SPEED |
| Snap / re-entry | A hard, short turn on the very top: back foot pushes the tail round, spray flies | On a steep, critical part near the lip | SPEED + LIP |
| Cutback (roundhouse) | Carve back toward the breaking part of the wave, then turn again to go down the line | When you have outrun the power, or want to reset | SPEED |
| Floater | Go up and let the board ride ON TOP of the breaking lip, weight on the back foot, then come down with it | On a section breaking ahead of you | SPEED + SECTION |
| Foam climb | Like a floater, but on the foam ball | Just after a section has broken | SPEED + SECTION |
| Tube / barrel | Stall (slow a little) or trim high, crouch, touch the wall with one hand, wave curls over you | On a hollow wave, deep in the pocket | LIP + hollow wave |
| Layback | Lean way back and drag a hand, a big carve | Big turns, power sections | SPEED |
| Tail slide / fin release | Back foot pushes so the fins pop out and the tail slides sideways over the lip, then catch the wave again | Top of the wave, a small steep spot | SPEED + LIP |
| Air / air reverse | Fast approach, hit a ramp-like lip, leave the wave, turn the board 180 deg in the air, land and keep riding | Steep crumbly lip | SPEED + LIP |
| Alley-oop / full rotation | Air that goes backward (alley-oop) or spins 360 deg | Same, higher level | SPEED + LIP |
| Grabs (indy, stalefish) | Hand grabs the rail while in the air (indy = toe-side rail between feet, stalefish = heel-side rail behind back leg) | During an air | SPEED + LIP |
| Hang five / hang ten | Walk to the front of a longboard, five or ten toes over the nose | Slow, smooth, small wave | face (little speed) |
| Kick-out | Turn up and over the back of the wave, or jump off, to end the ride | End of ride | any |

A simple memory trick (my wording): **Bottom turn = fuel, top turn / snap = spray, cutback = reset, floater = go over the top, air = leave the wave.**

## 5. How a ride is structured and judged
WSL (World Surf League) judges give 0-10 per wave using these criteria: commitment and degree of difficulty, innovative/progressive moves, combination of major maneuvers, variety, and speed, power and flow. Five judges, top and bottom dropped, middle three averaged ([WSL: surf judging explained](https://worldsurfleague.com/posts/479058/surf-judging-explained), [WSL Surf 101](https://worldsurfleague.com/the-ultimate-surfer/surf-101)). Judges adjust to the day's waves, so a great ride on a small day still scores high. Critics say the formula is vague ([The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/surf/is-it-time-we-get-wsl-judging-criteria-that-actually-makes-sense/)).
- **Commitment**: going all in on a risky move (late take-off, deep tube, big air).
- **Difficulty**: critical section (the steepest part), tricky move.
- **Innovation**: new/progressive moves.
- **Combination**: several major moves linked in one wave.
- **Variety**: different moves, not the same snap five times.
- **Speed / power / flow**: fast, strong, smooth links with no stops.

Typical good ride shape (my summary): take-off (pop-up) -> bottom turn (set-up) -> a first big turn -> use speed to link a second and third move (cutback, floater, snap) -> finish on the best section (air, tube) -> kick-out. Start fast, end big.

## 6. Learner progression (the ladder)
Order is the same across coaching sources; timeframes come from one commercial planner and are rough ([Griply learn-to-surf plan](https://griply.app/goal-plans/fun-and-relaxation/learn-to-surf), [Rapture Camps](https://www.rapturecamps.com/learn-to-surf/surf-fundamentals)).
1. Paddle and pop-up on land, then in the water.
2. Prone (lying) rides, then standing on whitewater (about weeks 2-4).
3. Green (unbroken) waves, straight ride (about 1-3 months).
4. Angle across the face, trim, a gentle bottom turn.
5. Ride the face with turns and pumping (about 3-6 months).
6. Top turn, cutback, then snaps and floaters (my knowledge: often a year or more).
7. Tube, airs and rotations: years.
Board ladder (my knowledge): foamie -> funboard/longboard -> fish -> shortboard.

## 7. Beginner realities
What a beginner really does on whitewater: paddle hard, feel the foam push, pop up (often late), stand stiff on the board, go straight to the beach, wobble, maybe angle a little, fall, or step off in the shallows.

Common mistakes (consistent across [Rapture Camps: beginner mistakes](https://www.rapturecamps.com/learn-to-surf/surf-fundamentals/beginner-mistakes/), [Rapture Camps: take-off mistakes](https://www.rapturecamps.com/learn-to-surf/take-off/take-off-mistakes), [SurferToday: pearling](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/how-to-avoid-nosediving-pearling-in-surfing)):
| Mistake | What happens | Fall look |
|---|---|---|
| Looking down at feet | Head drops, weight goes forward, nose digs | Pearl: board nose-dives, surfer flies over the front |
| Weight too far forward / paddling too far forward | Same | Pearl, tumble |
| Standing too far back ("bogging") | Nose rises, board slows | Stall, falls backwards off the tail |
| Late take-off | Wave already broken or too steep | Free-fall, belly flop, tumbled in the foam |
| Early or slow pop-up (knee stage, crawling) | Not standing in time | Slides off the side |
| Stiff straight legs, arms down | No balance | Wobble then sideways flop |
| Catching a rail (edge digs in) | Board stops dead on one side | Sudden side flop |
| Big wave too fast | Can't keep up | Washing machine (rolled underwater), board boomerang on the leash |
Falls look funny but are small: arms flap, board shoots, a splash, a head pops up laughing. Beginners usually fall flat, not dramatically.

## 8. What our game does today (from the brief)
Allowed now: carves, snaps, cutbacks, floaters, airs with spins and grabs, bottom turns, barrels. NPCs: snaps, cutbacks, floaters, bottom turns, airs.
The complaint (small waves block snaps/tricks) suggests our rules gate moves by wave size or wave "level" rather than by speed and section. I did not read the code (not allowed to edit; reading was out of scope), so this is my guess about the cause.

## Ideas for our game
Ranked by value for effort. "Step" = roadmap step; all of these fit Step 5 (polish and depth) or later.

1. **Gate by speed and flow, not by wave size (Step 5, top priority).** Let every green wave (Intermediate and up) allow every turn move: snaps, cutbacks, floaters, tail slides, small airs. Make each move need a minimum speed and a "steep section" marker instead of a minimum wave height. Small waves just get smaller spray and lower airs (scale effect size by wave height). This directly fixes Ido's feedback.
2. **Add pumping as the speed mechanic for small waves (Step 5).** A rhythmic input (alternate lean left/right, or press and release a key in time with the face) adds speed. On small waves speed decays, so the player must pump to reach the snap/air speed. On big waves speed is free, so pumping matters less. This also makes small waves feel skillful, not boring.
3. **Sections on the wave as opportunities (Step 5).** Mark short "critical" spots on the peeling wave (a steeper lip, a crumbling section, a foam ball ahead). Snap/tail slide/air need LIP; floater needs SECTION ahead; barrel needs a hollow section. Show them with a subtle glow or foam cue. This ties into the "wave sections" plan in [long-rides.md](long-rides.md).
4. **Per-level move menu (Step 5):**
   | Level | Allowed | Speed/flow need | Fun failure |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Beginner whitewater | Pop-up (graded), straight ride, small angle, kick-out, hang-five bonus on a longboard if the player chooses to walk forward | Speed from the foam is constant; no flow needed | Pearl, stand too late, bog (too far back), wobble flop |
   | Intermediate green (1-3 ft) | Trim, pump, bottom turn, top turn, small snap, cutback, floater at crumbly sections, tail slide, small air for skilled | Snap about 40% flow, floater needs section, air needs 70% flow + lip | Catch a rail, bog out of speed on the flat, nose dig on floater landing |
   | Pro barreling (6 ft+) | Everything, plus barrel, big airs, rotations, grabs, layback | High speed given free; the skill is timing and committing | Over-the-falls, lip hit, washing machine, barrel close-out |
5. **Commitment risk/reward (Step 5).** Make moves cost more if they are riskier: a late take-off, deep barrel or air with spin gives a bigger score but a higher wipeout chance. This matches the WSL "commitment / difficulty" idea and gives the player real choices.
6. **Score with real judging words (Step 5).** On the score card, show the six WSL words: Speed, Power, Flow, Variety, Combo, Commitment. Repeat the same move and Variety goes down; link three different moves and Combo goes up. This teaches variety, which is what real judges reward. Great for later surf contest events ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) parking lot).
7. **Fun, realistic failure states (Step 5).** Pearl (nose dig, flip over the front), bog (stall at the back), rail catch (side flop), over-the-falls (thrown by the lip), late-drop belly flop, board boomerang on the leash, washing machine. Each ties to a cause: looking down = pearl, too far back = bog, too slow into a floater = pearl on landing. Show the cause in a one-line toast ("You looked down!") so the player learns.
8. **NPC rules match the player (Step 5).** NPCs pick moves by their skill and the wave's sections: beginners only go straight and wobble; improvers trim and bottom-turn; advanced snap and floater at sections; pros air and tube. On a small wave, advanced NPCs should still snap and tail-slide, just smaller. Add small-wave "pumping" visuals for them (S-curves) so spectators see skill on small days.
9. **Board choice matters (Step 5/6).** Longboard on a small wave = nose-ride bonus; fish = loose fun on small waves; shortboard on a mushy wave = loses speed, bonus when steep. See [boards-and-tricks.md](boards-and-tricks.md).
10. **Teaching moment: Surf Coach / lessons area (later).** Follow the real ladder as a mini-tutorial: pop-up -> whitewater -> green -> bottom turn -> cutback. Unlock one new move per ladder rung for both the player and your students. Fits the unchecked "Lessons area" item in the roadmap.
11. **Barrel as the "big wave reward" (later).** Keep barrels exclusive to hollow, bigger waves (Pro / Lagoon / a late Surf-Ranch-style pool). Make tube riding a stall-and-hold mechanic with a time bonus and a close-out risk.
12. **Show maneuver names (any step).** Pop up the name ("SNAP!", "FLOATER!", "AIR REVERSE!") with the power/size scaled to wave height so even small moves feel cool.
